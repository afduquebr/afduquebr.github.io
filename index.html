I work where collider physics meets machine learning: models that classify jets, learn what ordinary collisions look like, and flag the ones that aren’t.

At the Laboratoire de Physique de Clermont-Auvergne I am part of the ATLAS search for emerging jets, a possible signature of a dark sector, and I build the anomaly-detection and tagging tools around it.

Selected work

  1. Emerging jets

    An ATLAS search for jets whose tracks start away from the collision point, using large-radius reclustered jets and large-radius tracking.

  2. Dark-VAE

    A variational autoencoder trained on ordinary jets, so that jets from new physics show up as the ones it cannot reconstruct.

  3. JetFlow

    A machine-learning model of jets for ATLAS, learning their distribution directly from the data.

  4. Boosted tagger

    A multiclass tagger for boosted jets built on the Particle Transformer, telling W, Z, top and Higgs jets from QCD.
